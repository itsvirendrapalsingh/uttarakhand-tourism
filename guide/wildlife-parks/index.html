Wildlife and national parks

Corbett, Rajaji, Nanda Devi, Valley of Flowers and sanctuaries

Home / Plan / Wildlife and national parks

Uttarakhand has national parks, tiger reserves and sanctuaries. Rules on permits and safaris change often; use official sources.

Jim Corbett National Park

Tiger reserve in the Kumaon foothills. Per the official site (corbettgov.org): Jhirna, Dhela and Garjia are open all year; Bijrani is open 15 October to 30 June; Dhikala, Durgadevi, Sonanadi and Pakhro 15 November to 15 June. Book only on the official site (booking.corbettgov.org); it warns about scam booking sites. Valid ID must match the ID uploaded at booking, and mobile phones are not permitted in tourism zones.

Rajaji National Park

Forest reserve near Haridwar and Rishikesh, known for elephants. Safaris run from a few gates.

Nanda Devi and Valley of Flowers National Parks

A UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Valley of Flowers opened on 1 June 2026 and was reported open to 31 October; the core of Nanda Devi National Park is restricted.

Gangotri National Park

High-altitude park; permits are needed for treks inside, including Gaumukh. Secondary 2026 guides report a daily cap of 150 and a mandatory guide, insurance and medical certificate; confirm with the Uttarkashi forest office.

Govind Pashu Vihar and Binsar

Govind Pashu Vihar covers the Har Ki Dun and Kedarkantha area. Binsar Wildlife Sanctuary near Almora offers forest walks with an entry fee.

Responsible viewing

  • Keep a safe distance; never feed animals.
  • Stay in the vehicle on safari.
  • Do not expect a sighting; tigers are rarely seen.
  • Use only licensed guides and official booking channels.

Frequently asked questions

Can I book a Corbett safari on arrival?

Usually not. Book online in advance through the official channel.
